# Changelog


## 0.2.59
- Pace guard follows the measured burn: a lane's paced slots were priced only by its estimated burn per run (`targetShare = required / E`), so when the runs on it cost more than the estimate (max-effort reviews on lanes calibrated by cheaper doer runs) the cap stayed high while the lane burned several times faster than its plan. Live 2026-10-21: the two paced Meta lanes measured 2.1%/h against 0.55%/h required at cap 4. Now an ahead lane with measured > required has its slots scaled by required/measured (floor 0.1; `laneVerdicts[].rateCorrection` on /capacity). Lanes on or behind plan, unmeasured lanes and lanes within plan are unchanged.

## 0.2.58
- Runnable kept/review demand: kept agents (`roles.keepAgentIds`) and routed review agents (`reviewerRouting.agentIds`) now count actual queued heartbeat runs via the host aggregate `agents.getQueuedRunCounts` (new manifest capability `agents.queued_counts.read`) instead of assigned open `todo`/`in_progress` cards. A Reviewer holding hundreds of idle chat-review cards with one run in flight has demand ~1, not hundreds, so stale review backlog can no longer claim the whole fleet target and strand routed agents at their running floors. Routed (non-review) agents keep issue demand. When the queued-run read is denied or unavailable, kept/review agents degrade to `queued 0` with `demandSource: 'heartbeat-runs-unavailable'` -- never falling back to assigned cards -- and grant no new slots. Every `/caps` entry reports `demandSource` (`issues` | `heartbeat-runs` | `heartbeat-runs-unavailable`) so consumers can see why an agent got its allocation. Requires the host capability; until the host ships it, kept/review demand reads running-only (still correct: no stale backlog claims capacity).

## 0.2.57
- Burn accounts spend to exhaustion: the lane feed marks an account `degraded` once any meter passes 90% (`healthReason: 'peak utilization ...'`), and only healthy accounts allocate, so burn lanes stopped at 90% and left the rest of their week unspent. A burn account degraded ONLY by that floor now reads healthy (with `feedHealth: 'degraded'` on `/capacity`) until its weekly meter reaches 0.995 or its five-hour bucket reaches 0.99. Degraded for any other reason (recent failures, cooldowns), exhausted, unavailable and non-burn accounts are unchanged. The lane parser keeps the optional feed `healthReason`.

## 0.2.56
- Kept-agent caps with burn mode: the pool pace cap (`paceGuard.pools[].cap`) now bounds only the pool's paced accounts. Burn-mode accounts are exempt from the pace guard, so their slots add on top (`burnAccountIds`). Before, a kept Reviewer serving from Meta burn lanes (~270 slots) was clamped to the 4-slot cap of the two paced lanes. Unavailable burn accounts still add nothing, so the cap bounds the pool again once burn lanes are spent.

## 0.2.55
- Cheapest capable model (owner rule 2026-10-20, every provider and account): `pacing.climbForSpend` (default false) keeps each account on its cheapest capable rung; unused quota is spent through throughput (fleet C*), never by climbing to pricier rungs. True restores the old climb.

## 0.2.54
- Burn mode now raises the fleet concurrency target: a burn account's C* need uses its burn end (now + horizon), not its weekly reset.

## 0.2.53 — 2026-10-20

- Burn mode per provider account: new config `burnMode: { accounts: string[], horizonMinutes?: number (default 60) }`. An entry matches an account by id (`meta:meta-lane-3`), lane/accountKey (`meta-lane-3`), the feed's `label`/`email` when present (case-insensitive), or `provider:*` (every account of a provider); a leading `!` excludes and wins over positive entries (`["codex:*", "meta:*", "!rdugger@..."]`). A burn account paces to `min(regular reset, free-reset horizon, now + horizonMinutes)` so its required rate is maximal and it never reads ahead of plan; the pool pace guard leaves burn lanes out of its aggregation and never holds them (an all-burn pool is unpaced: `cap null`, `state 'burn'`); review-reserve holds never apply; routing sorts burn accounts first among the accounts that can serve (health, headroom, breakers, exclusions and exhaustion still apply). `/capacity` shows `burnMode: true` and `paceResetAtMs` on burn accounts and `paceGuard.burnAccounts`. The lane parser keeps optional feed `label`/`email`/`accountKey`. Validation rejects a non-array `accounts`, non-string or empty entries and a horizon below one minute. Absent/empty burnMode decides bit-for-bit as before (regression test compares hook decisions, shadow log, accounts, pace pools and review reserve).
- Per-lane pace guard: a pool is no longer judged on the mean plan position of its lanes. Each lane is judged on its own position; the pool cap is the sum of per-lane allowances (an ahead lane contributes its paced slots, a lane on or behind plan its full fair share `max(1, slots)`), and only lanes ahead of their own plan are held at the cap (`pacedAccountIds`, state `lanes-paced`). A spent lane (claude-lane-3 at 99%) no longer caps a lane far behind plan (claude-lane-4 at 11% with 4.6 days left) at one run. Single-lane pools are unchanged.
- Whole-percent meters: vendor meters tick in whole percents, so a flat 60-minute window read 0.00%/h at ~0.4%/h real burn. A per-account tick log (meter changes, 6h) lets a flat window extend back to the last tick, bounded by one quantum over the time since that tick (`rateEstimate: 'tick-extended'`); with no tick in 6h and runs in flight on the account the rate reads the one-quantum bound (`'tick-bound'`), never exactly 0. A positive window rate, thin data (null) and an idle flat account (0) are unchanged.
- Reviewer capacity across providers: `reviewer.providers` now defaults to `reviewReserve.providers` when the operator set it (else `['claude']`); an explicit `reviewer.providers` still wins. With `reviewReserve.providers: ['claude', 'meta']`, reviewer slots and `qualifiedAccountIds` count Meta and Claude lanes. The manifest no longer injects a `['claude']` default for `reviewer.providers`, so the fallback is reachable.
- Reviewer routing (opt-in): new `reviewerRouting: { enabled?, agentIds?, arms: [{ provider, model, effort? }] }`. For a run of a listed agent (default `reviewReserve.agentIds`, which defaults to `roles.keepAgentIds`) the hook picks an arm instead of answering `keep`: only healthy, non-excluded (weekly-exhausted), non-guarded accounts with headroom above the reserve (reactive lanes on health) that serve the arm's model qualify; role `excludeFamilies`, arm breakers (closed only) and, when enforced, credential readiness apply. Ranking: plan headroom before credit headroom, burn-mode accounts (the tick's identity-aware verdict, including label/email exclusions), the pool most behind its pace plan (mean `planAhead`), most remaining quota per hour to reset, arm order, then the least-loaded lane. The decision is recorded enforced on the chosen account (burn, pacing and the review reserve attribute it there). No qualifying arm falls back to `keep` (or normal routing for an unpinned agent). Accounts serving a routing arm count as reviewer-eligible.
- `/api/capacity` `reviewer.routing`: `{ enabled, agentIds, arms, burnModeTokens, provider, arm, accountId, reason, preview, last }` (tick preview plus the hook's last outcome); `{ enabled: false }` when unconfigured.
- Absent `reviewerRouting`: pinned reviewers keep exactly as before (regression test). Tests: `test/reviewer-routing.test.mjs`.

## 0.2.52 — 2026-10-19

- Pending arm-id disambiguation: the pending enforced path passes the decided served id (`wouldModel`) into the arm-id dash-strip, so a dynamic arm whose served id ends in an effort word (`nemotron-3-ultra`) keys verbatim (`nemotron-3-ultra:max`) exactly like its backfilled record, while operator armIds (`foo-bar-high`) keep stripping to the colon form (`foo-bar:high`). A served id kept verbatim also skips the arm-id-suffix effort fallback (its trailing effort word is part of the name) and defaults like the observed path instead. The pool anchor index passes the same served-id context when indexing ladder arms. ArmId syntax alone cannot distinguish the two spellings, so records without served-id context keep the previous strip behavior.

## 0.2.49 — 2026-10-17

- Arm-aware burn calibration: burn per run is measured per arm (model+effort) per calibration pool, not per account. Pooled meter deltas split across the arms that ran in the span by responsibility (each arm's prior burn times its run count, scaled to the observed delta); a pure-span sample measures its arm exactly. Pricing follows the current ledger run mix over the pacing window, with cost-anchor priors for never-measured arms, so switching doers from an expensive arm to a cheap arm raises slots within one tick of ledger evidence instead of waiting for quantized meter quanta.
- A dominant-arm change segments old-mix measurements immediately: the pooled EWMA restarts and members' persisted individual estimates are discarded until fresh per-account evidence re-accumulates (a first-seen mix that reprices the pool by more than 25% segments too, so an upgrade onto an already-switched fleet recovers on the first tick). Arm-less runs (no decision, no model) price as their own `unattributed` segment rather than vanishing from the denominator.
- Ledger decisions record effort; the shadow log exposes the decided `armId` (parity golden regenerated: only the newly visible arm id differs).
- Review-round hardening (3/5 → fix): arm keys trust the decided arm id (and effort) only for enforced hook decisions; every other record keys by the model that actually ran (unknown/pending models bucket as unattributed); unmeasured-arm priors rescale off the measured arms' anchors with measurement-era anchors recorded per arm (a switch onto lanes that no longer serve the old arm still reprices); the pooled estimate is priced from the carried value first and restarts only on a fresh sample, and a dominant-arm flip segments only past a 60% share with a >25% reprice so near-even mixes never flap smoothing; fresh counter-backed estimates re-base onto the mix-priced group baseline on a segmented tick.
- Regression: an expensive-to-cheap arm switch raises concurrency slots within one tick (`test/arm-calibration.test.mjs` pure helpers plus `test/arm-tick.test.mjs` driving the real tick: one-tick reprice and segmentation, flat-meter persistence, noise hysteresis, executed-model attribution). Full suite: 781 green.
